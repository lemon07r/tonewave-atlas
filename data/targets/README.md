# Reference target inputs

Published research curves; redistributed with attribution; remove on request. Copied unchanged from
`jaakkopasanen/AutoEq`, commit `7ae0f56d53074872b028649617a22bbb4232feb7`, `targets/`.
AutoEq's software MIT licence does not establish separate rights in the underlying research curves.

- `Harman in-ear 2019.csv`: Harman's in-ear target, 2019 revision as distributed by AutoEq.
  Original research: Olive, Welti and Khonsaripour, “A Statistical Model that Predicts Listeners'
  Preference Ratings of In-Ear Headphones: Part 1—Listening Test Results and Acoustic Measurements”,
  AES 143, paper 9840 (2017), <https://aes.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=19237>.
  AutoEq's revision history: <https://github.com/jaakkopasanen/AutoEq/issues/85>.
- `Diffuse field 5128.csv`: Brüel & Kjær, “Free-field and diffuse-field characteristics of Brüel &
  Kjær High-frequency HATS Type 5128”, ITU-T SG12 Contribution 257 (2018); see also Type 5128 family
  product data BP2573, Fig. 3, <https://www.bksv.com/-/media/literature/Product-Data/bp2573.ashx>.

Via AutoEq, <https://github.com/jaakkopasanen/AutoEq>. These are reference shapes, not universal
preference or calibration guarantees. Harman over-ear 2018 is intentionally excluded: its GRAS
measurement basis is not matched to our headphone partitions.
